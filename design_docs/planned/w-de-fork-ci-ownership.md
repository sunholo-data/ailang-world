## Problem Statement

The attended DE-FORK commit removed World's copied mission driver and routing test, but two World-owned verification entry points still invoke them. The macOS CI job fails extracting watchdog functions from the deleted driver; the Go verification script exits before Go build/test at the deleted routing test. This is a shared ownership-transition gap: deleting the implementation did not retire its consumers or update the CI job inventory assertion.

The consequence is larger than one failing check. At dev HEAD `565d0b2` the `go host build + test gate` job exits 127 at the routing line, which sits BEFORE `go build ./...`, the focused evidence manifest, `go test ./... -count=1` and the race leg (V17). Every Go product assertion is therefore suspended in CI, not failing; a real Go regression landing on dev today would be invisible. The repair is a gate restoration, and its success claim must be that the product checks run again, not merely that the jobs turn green.

The repair must honor the new centralized owner. Recreating the deleted files or redirecting CI into a mutable sibling checkout would reinstate the dependency being removed. World should verify its own mission inputs and product gates, and explicitly decline to certify the installed shared runtime. Revision 1 stopped at the question of whether World could also retire its own obsolete fleet-comparison diagnostic; `D-WORLD-34` has now answered that (V14), and this revision carries the answer through the design.
